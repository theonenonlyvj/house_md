# Naming: "house_md" — recommendation

## Recommendation: keep it (Vijay's call, 2:55pm) — and say it smart

Keep "house_md" everywhere today: stage, form, video, repo. The name owns the wink,
and a one-day hackathon project is the safest possible place to enjoy it. Keep the
wink trademark-wary with four habits:

1. **Style it as software.** Lowercase snake_case in print; "house, M.D." spoken.
   It reads as a repo name, and it is one.
2. **Never borrow the show's expression.** No logo, no cast likeness, no quoted
   dialogue, no theme music, no "based on the TV series." Trademark trouble comes
   from borrowed expression, and we borrowed none.
3. **Let the archetype work unnamed.** A brilliant diagnostic team arguing at a
   whiteboard is medicine's own furniture: grand rounds, morbidity and mortality
   conferences, tumor boards. The show borrowed team differential diagnosis from
   medicine; we borrowed it back.
4. **If a judge asks about the name:** "It's a wink at the cultural archetype of
   team-based differential diagnosis. The empty chair is the part he never had."

## Risk note (honest, low)

"House M.D." marks live in entertainment classes with NBCUniversal. A non-commercial
hackathon project in a software/clinical class, using none of the show's expression,
carries low practical risk today. The moment this becomes a company, rename before
incorporation and marketing spend; the alternates below are directions, not cleared
marks — nobody has run a trademark search on any of them.

## Three alternates (only if the team later wants one)

1. **Curbside** — the "curbside consult" is the real clinical term for informally
   grabbing a specialist's brain; the product formalizes exactly that. Warm,
   insider-credible, and it keeps the rural story (the curbside most doctors can't
   walk to).
2. **Consilium** — Latin for council. Clean, clinical, ownable; loses the warmth.
3. **The Differential** — names the artifact on the board. Descriptive and safe;
   hardest to trademark for the same reason.
